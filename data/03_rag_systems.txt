Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is a technique that combines a retrieval system with a
language model to produce answers grounded in external documents rather than relying only on the
model's training data. Instead of asking a language model to answer purely from memory, a RAG
pipeline first searches a knowledge base for relevant passages and then feeds those passages to
the model as context.

A typical RAG pipeline has four stages. First, documents are split into smaller chunks, since
whole documents are usually too large to embed or fit into a model's context window effectively.
Second, each chunk is converted into a vector embedding that captures its meaning. Third, when a
user asks a question, the question itself is embedded and compared against the stored chunk
vectors using a similarity measure, and the most relevant chunks are retrieved. Fourth, the
retrieved chunks are inserted into a prompt along with the original question, and a language model
generates a final answer grounded in that retrieved context.

RAG is useful because it lets a system answer questions about private, recent, or very specific
information without retraining the underlying model. It also reduces hallucination, since the
model is encouraged to base its answer on retrieved evidence rather than inventing facts. The
quality of a RAG system depends heavily on chunking strategy, embedding quality, and how the
retrieval step ranks candidate passages.
